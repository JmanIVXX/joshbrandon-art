## 1. What were the top-selling indie / small-studio / AA Steam releases of 2024, 2025 and 2026 YTD?

### Takeaway
Each year produced a few $50M+ Steam outliers from small or mid-size teams. In 2024 these were Palworld, Helldivers 2 (AA), Manor Lords and Balatro. In 2025 they were Schedule I ($151M), R.E.P.O. ($147M), PEAK ($87M), Silksong ($75M) and Escape from Duckov ($53M). So far in 2026 they are Slay the Spire 2 ($141.7M by 9 July), Subnautica 2 ($133.6M, AA), Meccha Chameleon (10M+ units at $5.99, $50–73M) and Mewgenics (~$46–61M), with Wardogs (AA, ~3.5M units) close behind. The 2025–26 outliers lean heavily toward cheap ($5–10) online co-op ("friendslop") and roguelike/roguelite designs. Below them sits a broad tier of $1–10M breakouts, mostly job sims, idle games, co-op party games and narrative games. That tier is better evidence of what a "typical" success looks like.

### Cited Findings

#### How the trackers measure (read first)
- GameDiscoverCo publishes **gross revenue estimates, "not actual figures"**. Its year-end 2024 chart ranked by units, and Simon Carless "didn't share any specific sales estimates" for it — [Game World Observer, 19 Dec 2024](https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/12/19/top-steam-and-console-games-2024-copies-sold-revenue); [WN Hub on GameDiscoverCo H1 2024](https://wnhub.io/news/stores-and-publishing/item-44658)
- **Alinea Analytics and Video Game Insights (VGI) differ by method.** For H1 2026, Alinea estimated Steam gross revenue at **$11.1B (+14.5% YoY, a record half-year)**, while VGI put it at **$5.36B (−5% YoY)**. Per the coverage, Alinea counts all transactions including free-to-play, and VGI counts only paid copy sales — [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/23714/steam-earns-167-trillion-in-first-half-alone); [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/stores-and-publishing/item-51420); [GameDev.net](https://gamedev.net/news/alinea-steam-revenue-reaches-111bn-marking-highest-half-year-on-record-r4426/)
- **Gamalytic publishes ranges.** For example, Schedule I is estimated at **9M copies (range 6.3M–11.7M)**, i.e. ±30% — [Gamalytic: Schedule I](https://gamalytic.com/game/3164500). Raijin labels its Mewgenics estimate "medium confidence" — [Raijin](https://raijin.gg/app/686060/Mewgenics)
- **Trackers can disagree 2x on the same title.** For R.E.P.O.'s first <3 weeks (early 2025), Gamalytic estimated ~3.1M copies and VG Insights ~1.5M. Semiwork has not disclosed sales — [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/indie/item-47358)
- **"Indie" is defined differently by different trackers.** VGI counted Black Myth: Wukong as indie and put indie at **48% of $8.3B** Steam full-game revenue (1 Jan–30 Sep 2024), with indies at **98.9% of 13,007 products released**. Alinea put indie at **~25%** of 2025 Steam revenue. Secondary coverage notes that the headline "indie share" "depends entirely on where you draw the line" — [Game World Observer on VGI, 16 Oct 2024](https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/10/16/indie-games-revenue-steam-vs-aaa-titles-vg-insights); [Shane the Gamer stats roundup](https://www.shanethegamer.com/research/indie-games-statistics/)
- **Valve's "Best of" tiers are official but coarse.** Games are sorted randomly within each tier. The 2025 Top Sellers list initially covered 1 Jan–1 Dec 2025, with a planned update in early January 2026 — [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/steams-best-selling-games-of-2025-reveal-2-indie-hits-that-made-it-big-3298767/); [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Steam-ranks-its-best-selling-games-for-2025-with-AAA-and-indie-surprises-making-the-charts.1193939.0.html)

## 2. Which genres account for the most indie hits ($1M+) and moderate successes ($300k–$1M) in 2024–2026?

### Takeaway
No source captured here gives a genre breakdown of games specifically above $1M or within $300k–$1M. The best available proxy is Chris Zukowski's count of games reaching 1,000+ Steam reviews:
- **2025** (608 games): Narrative 51, Simulation 43, Horror 39, RPG 28, Idle/Incremental 27, Roguelike 22, Management 19
- **2024** (445 games): Horror 47 was #1 and Narrative 30 was #2

Together with GameDiscoverCo's finding that roguelite/roguelike tags surged among 50k+ copy sellers (2021→2025) and Alinea's finding that co-op had the highest average revenue in 2025, this indicates three things:
- The *middle class* of successes is led by narrative, simulation, horror, idle and roguelike games.
- The *top end* is led by co-op and roguelike/roguelite.
- Only ~1.5–3% of releases reach these levels.

### Cited Findings
- **Zukowski, 2025 cohort** (published 27 Jan 2026). He counted **20,282** Steam releases in 2025, of which **608** reached 1,000+ reviews.
  - Data came from VG Insights, sampled 4 Jan 2026. He assigns one genre per game by hand and filters out Chinese-only games.
  - Genre counts: **Narrative 51, Simulation 43, Horror 39, RPG 28, Idle/Incremental 27, Roguelike 22, Management 19**.
  - This was the first time Horror dropped to #3; it had led in prior years. 9 of the top 10 genres matched the 2024 list.
  - His caveat: many games earn **$150K+ and get Daily Deals with as few as 500 reviews**.
  - Sources: [How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/01/27/what-the-hell-happened-in-2025/); [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/a-terrifying-20-282-games-were-released-on-steam-in-2025-and-just-608-managed-to-get-1-000-reviews-expert-finds-we-might-be-in-a-bit-of-an-indie-golden-age/)

  - **445 of 18,239** releases reached 1,000 reviews, a **2.44%** rate. Another citation gives 18,234 releases.

  - Sources: [How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/01/15/what-the-hell-happened-in-2024/); [presskit.gg](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/indie-game-marketing-timeline)

### Inferences
- **Derived hit rates (1,000+ reviews):**
  - 2025: 608 ÷ 20,282 = **3.0%**, vs 2024: 445 ÷ 18,239 = **2.44%**.
  - The hit count rose +36.6% (445 → 608) while releases rose only +11.2% (18,239 → 20,282). By this proxy, success got slightly *more* attainable in 2025. This matches Zukowski's "indie golden age" framing — [How To Market A Game, 4 Nov 2025](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/11/04/the-optimistic-case-that-indie-games-are-in-a-golden-age-right-now/)
- **Derived genre shares of the 1,000-review cohort.** The top 7 genres together = 229 ÷ 608 = **37.7%**, so ~62% of hits are spread across many smaller genres; the middle class is fragmented.

  | Genre | 2024 share | 2025 share | Change in count |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Narrative | 30 ÷ 445 = 6.7% | 51 ÷ 608 = **8.4%** | **+70%** |
  | Simulation | — | 7.1% | — |
  | Horror | 47 ÷ 445 = 10.6% | 6.4% | **−17%** while all hits grew 37% |
  | RPG | — | 4.6% | — |
  | Idle/Incremental | — | 4.4% | — |
  | Roguelike | — | 3.6% | — |
  | Management | — | 3.1% | — |
- **Derived horror supply.** Horror's 2024 hit rate implies ~47 ÷ 0.0181 ≈ **2,600 horror releases** in 2024: very high supply and low odds. Open World Survival Craft's 24.47% hit rate implies a far smaller, less crowded pool.
- **Derived $1M rate.** Alinea's ~300 ÷ ~20,000 = **~1.5%** of 2025 launches crossed $1M.
- **Mapping 1,000 reviews to the $300k "moderate success" line (low confidence).** Zukowski's rule of thumb is that 500 reviews can mean $150k+. If that scales roughly linearly, 1,000 reviews is about $300k or more. On that basis:
  - The 608-game 2025 cohort is a rough proxy for the study's "moderate success+" set.
  - 608 − ~300 games above $1M ≈ **~300 games in the ~$300k–$1M band in 2025**.
  - Valve's ~540 games above $250k in their first 30 days (2024) is the same order of magnitude as Zukowski's 445 for 2024, which is consistent with this mapping.
  - Review-to-revenue ratios vary widely with price, region and genre, so treat this mapping as illustrative only.
- **Most $100k+ earners are catalog titles.** Valve's 5,863 includes back-catalog games, whereas Gamalytic's 2025-release cohort implies ~8% × 12,732 ≈ **~1,019** new games above $100k by October (derived; an immature cohort). Most $100k+ earners in a given year are therefore older titles, and long-tail/catalog sales matter for reaching $300k lifetime.
- **Different metrics give different genre leaders.** The "genres of $1M+ hits" depend on the metric:
  - By **count** of moderate-plus successes: narrative, simulation, horror, idle and roguelike.
  - By **revenue concentration** at the top: co-op multiplayer and roguelike/roguelite.
  - By **hit *rate*** (odds per release): open-world survival craft looked best in 2024, and horror looked poor.

## 3. Which trends are rising, peaking or fading as of October 2026?

### Takeaway
Rising:
- **Co-op "friendslop"**, whose audience is still growing and has evolved toward deeper "co-op-only" and co-op extraction designs
- **First-person job/shop simulators**
- **Narrative** games
- **Roguelike/roguelite hybrids**
- **Ultra-low pricing ($5–10)** for viral social games

Peaking or saturating in supply:
- **Idle/incremental** (1,087 releases in 2026 by July)
- **Friendslop** in release-count terms, though Zukowski argues it is not oversaturated

Declining in share of hits:
- **Horror** (from #1 to #3)
- **City builders** (merging into tower defense)
- **Indie PvP multiplayer shooters** (hits "quickly die")
- **Mid-priced $25–55 indies**

## 4. What platform or market changes in 2024–2026 affect indie success on Steam?

### Takeaway
Steam keeps growing (record H1 2026 per Alinea) and a record 5,863 games earned >$100k in 2025. However, release volume and Next Fest volume rose faster than per-game attention:
- June 2026 Next Fest had **+66% demos** while top-10% follower gains fell **~25%**.
- New releases took only **~21%** of H1 2026 revenue.

Valve is moving discovery toward **personalized algorithms**: the June 2026 homepage redesign, a personalized Discounts & Events grid from Sept 2026, and fully algorithmic deal placement planned for early 2027. It expanded **Daily Deals** from 1 slot to 6 in 2025, and it **narrowed AI-disclosure rules** in Jan 2026, by which point ~20–30% of new releases disclosed generative AI.

Prices of top-selling new indies are trending **down**. No evidence was found that tariffs affected Steam pricing.

### Inferences
- **Crowding is pushing attention onto pre-launch momentum.** Next Fest demos grew +66% (to 4,382) while top-decile follower gains fell ~26% (derived: (163−121)/163). Demos launched before the fest earn ~2.5x the wishlists, so a Next Fest slot alone yields less in 2026 than in 2024–25, and early demos, momentum and virality matter more.
- **Catalog and post-launch visibility matter more than before.** New releases took only ~21% of H1 2026 revenue (~$2.3B of $11.1B, derived), and personalised deal surfaces are replacing curated ones (late 2026 to 2027). Accurate tagging and a strong discount and long-tail plan are therefore more important. Steam Page Analyzer and TechTimes headlines frame the new algorithm as rewarding "tag-rich" games, but this is not confirmed by Valve.
- **Daily Deals are a broad new lever for small games.** Six slots, 1,500 titles, 69% first-timers and +274% revenue, plus the record count of $100k earners, suggest Valve is spreading promotion more widely. This is a plausible tailwind for moderate ($100k–$1M) outcomes, though no causal study was found.
- **The price signal is mixed but points down at the viral end.** Viral co-op hits cluster at $5–10. Premium indies priced $25–30 still produced mega-hits (Slay the Spire 2 at $25, Mewgenics at ~$30), so "cheap" applies to the friendslop format, not to every genre.
- **AI disclosure is now common.** With ~20–30% of releases disclosing, disclosure is no longer rare. No evidence was captured on whether disclosure affects sales or reviews.
