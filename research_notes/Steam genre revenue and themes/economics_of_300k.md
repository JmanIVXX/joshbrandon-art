## 1. How rare is $300k? Share of Steam releases reaching $100k, $300k and $1M; median revenue; trend over time

### Takeaway
No source publishes a $300k share directly. Bracketing the published $100k and $1M shares gives a derived estimate: **about 3–4% of 2025 releases had reached $300k gross by the end of 2025** (on partial-year revenue). On a lifetime basis, **about 8–9% of paid games with at least one review reach it** (We Love It all-time data; Zukowski's 2019 indie cohort, older data). The typical game earns very little: the median 2025 release made about $249 (Gamalytic), and the median lifetime figure for paid games with at least one review is about $3,500 (We Love It). The number of games earning over $100k a year roughly doubled from 2020 to 2025 (Valve). Release volume grew at least as fast, so the *share* of new releases clearing $100k has been flat to falling: about 10.6% in 2020 and about 6.4% in 2025 in GameDiscoverCo's counts.

### Cited Findings

**The $100k threshold (2025 data)**
- **Valve (GDC, March 2026):** 5,863 games earned more than $100,000 on Steam in calendar 2025, an all-time record. Valve's Tom Giardino said only about 3,000 titles cleared $100k in 2020. Valve says it also tracks $500k and $1M thresholds and that "the trend lines stay really consistent" there, but none of the coverage I found gave $500k or $1M counts. The 5,863 counts *revenue earned in 2025 by any title*, including catalogue games released before 2025 — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/valve-says-5-836-titles-earned-over-100-000-on-steam-in-2025); [Gamereactor](https://www.gamereactor.eu/almost-6000-games-earned-more-than-100000-in-revenue-last-year-on-steam-valve-says-1689103/); [IndieGames.eu](https://www.indie-games.eu/valve-shares-new-steam-stats-at-gdc-over-5800-games-earned-100k-on-steam-in-2025/)
- **GameDiscoverCo's reading of Valve's slides (March 2026):** about 29% of the ~5,800 (≈1,700 games) were 2025 releases. That is about 8.5% of the roughly 20,000 games released in 2025 — [GameDiscoverCo, "What Steam's 2026 biz update tells us about the ecosystem"](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/what-steams-2026-biz-update-tells)
- **Kotaku (March 2026):** releases rose from 9,647 (2020) to 19,997 (2025), so the share crossing $100k "barely moved". Most of the 5,863 were not 2025 releases — [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/the-games-industry-reacts-to-valves-misleading-boast-about-how-more-games-are-making-money-on-steam-2000679108)
- **Mike Rose (No More Robots):** of the roughly 150,000 games on Steam, only ~4% earned over $100k in 2025 — [17173, 17 Mar 2026](https://news.17173.com/content/03172026/140211422.shtml). This is a secondary summary; I did not retrieve his original post.
- **GameDiscoverCo's own dataset:** titles grossing over $100k rose from ~950 (2020) to ~1,350 (2025). Total titles rose from ~9,000 to ~21,000. In GameDiscoverCo's words, "only 6.5% of the games we looked at in 2025 grossed >$100k" — [GameDiscoverCo newsletter](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/what-game-descriptions-tell-us-about) (attribution to this specific post unverified)
- **Gamalytic aggregates (October 2025, 2025 releases to date):**
  - ~8% of 2025 releases were estimated to have grossed over $100k.
  - 8,388 games (65.9%) earned under $1,000.
  - ~40% had not earned back the $100 Steam Direct fee.

  Sources: [DualShockers/OpenCritic](https://www.dualshockers.com/steam-games-gross-over-100-thousand-less-than-steam-listing-fees/); [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/over-5-000-games-released-on-steam-this-year-didnt-make-enough-money-to-recover-the-usd100-fee-to-put-a-game-on-valves-store-research-estimates/); [PC Gamer ("over 13,000 new games ... this year so far")](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/with-over-13-000-new-games-on-steam-this-year-so-far-over-a-third-of-them-havent-even-made-enough-to-break-even-on-valves-submission-fee/)
- **Copies sold:** only 1.8% of 2025 releases exceeded 100,000 copies sold (Gamalytic data) — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/analysts-report-drop-in-game-revenue-on-steam-despite-growing-number-of-releases)

**The $1M threshold**
- **Alinea Analytics (December 2025):**

  - Total Steam revenue was ≈$17.7B (1 Jan–19 Dec 2025).
  - Indie games took ≈$4.4–4.5B of that (≈25%).

  Sources: [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Indie-games-accounted-for-25-of-Steam-s-revenue-in-2025.1189429.0.html); [wnhub](https://wnhub.io/news/analytics/item-49645)
- **Valve 2024 Year in Review:** more than 200 new releases passed $1M in *first-month* gross revenue, up 15% on 2023. Valve defines new-release revenue as the first 30 days after release plus pre-purchases — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/2024-was-steams-best-year-ever-of-users-buying-newly-released-games-but-i-wouldnt-celebrate-the-end-of-the-forever-game-era-just-yet/); [Game World Observer, 14 Mar 2025](https://gameworldobserver.com/2025/03/14/steam-first-time-devs-games-revenue-2024-recap)

**Methodology caveats**
- **Gamalytic:** has no access to developers' Steamworks dashboards. Its per-game estimates are described as "mostly within a 30–50% margin of error", and some of its distribution charts are premium-only — [game-developers.org](https://game-developers.org/2025-steam-game-revenue-distribution); [Gamalytic](https://gamalytic.com/blog/steam-revenue-infographic) (attribution of the margin-of-error quote unverified)
- **We Love It:** relies entirely on a review multiplier, covers premium (paid) games only, and excludes games with zero reviews — [VGTimes](https://vgtimes.com/gaming-news/164749-steam-revenue-report-shows-the-top-1-of-games-earn-84.5-of-revenue.html); [We Love It](https://weloveit.io/developers/steam-revenue-report/)
- **Not comparable:** the "8%" (Gamalytic, 2025 releases) and Valve's 5,863 (all titles earning in 2025) use different denominators, revenue definitions and time windows — [ResetEra discussion of the Valve stats](https://www.resetera.com/threads/valve-gdc2026-5-863-games-earned-100k-in-2025-on-steam-more-stats.1460089/page-3); [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/the-games-industry-reacts-to-valves-misleading-boast-about-how-more-games-are-making-money-on-steam-2000679108)

### Inferences
- **Derived: share of games reaching $300k.**
  - **Method.** I assume the upper tail of the revenue distribution follows a power law between two published points:
    - P(>x) = P₁·(x/x₁)^(−α)
    - α = ln(P₁/P₂) / ln(x₂/x₁)
  - **Results:**

| Population / window | Inputs | α | P(>$300k) | P(>$1M) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 releases, partial-year revenue (2025 data) | 8% >$100k (Gamalytic); 1.5% >$1M (Alinea: 300 / 20,000) | 0.73 | **≈3.6%** | 1.5% (input) |
| Same | 6.5% >$100k (GameDiscoverCo); 1.5% >$1M | 0.64 | **≈3.2%** | 1.5% |
| Same | 8.5% >$100k (GameDiscoverCo's reading of Valve's slides); 1.5% >$1M | 0.75 | **≈3.7%** | 1.5% |
| 2019 indie cohort, lifetime (older data) | 8.5% ≥$250k; 4% ≥$1M (Zukowski) | 0.54 | **≈7.7%** | 4% (input) |
| All-time paid games with ≥1 review, lifetime (Aug 2026) | p90 = $236,184; p99 = $15.87M (We Love It) | 0.55 | **≈8.8%** | ≈4.5% |

  - **Reading the table.** About 3.2–3.7% of ~20,000 2025 releases means roughly 650–750 games had reached $300k by the end of 2025. That is about 1 in 27–31 releases, against about 1 in 11–13 on a lifetime basis among games that got at least one review.
  - **Why the two figures differ.** Three factors explain part of the gap:
    - Measurement window: months on sale versus lifetime.
    - Denominator: We Love It excludes zero-review games.
    - Possibly a real decline over time.

    These estimates are only as good as the input estimates, which carry 30–50% per-game error.
- **Derived: share of each year's releases grossing over $100k.**
  - GameDiscoverCo's counts give 950 / 9,000 = 10.6% in 2020 and 1,350 / 21,000 = 6.4% in 2025. Because volume rose faster than winners, the share has fallen.
  - Valve's catalogue counts set against release counts (Kotaku) give 3,000 / 9,647 = 31% in 2020 and 5,863 / 19,997 = 29% in 2025. These ratios mix catalogue earners with new releases, so they are not success rates. They only show that the absolute number of $100k earners grew roughly in line with release volume.
- **Derived: the Gamalytic snapshot is partial-year.** 8,388 / 0.659 ≈ 12,700 games in the October 2025 snapshot, which matches "over 13,000 new games ... this year so far" (PC Gamer). Many of these games had been on sale for only weeks or months, so the October 2025 shares understate lifetime outcomes.
- **Mean versus median.** The 2025 mean of $358,900 is about 1,440 times the median of $249. Means such as VG Insights' $120k–$209k per indie game are pulled up by outliers and are not useful for planning.
- **Proxy for indie games.** Indie titles make up the large majority of release volume (VG Insights uses a broad tag-based definition), so the all-release base rates are a reasonable proxy for indie base rates. I found no 2024–2026 indie-only $300k figure.

### Gaps
- No source publishes a direct share of games above $300k. Valve's $500k and $1M counts were mentioned but not published in the coverage I found.
- I found no 2026 year-to-date distribution data (for example, H1 2026 releases by revenue band).
- Gamalytic's exact $250k, $500k and $1M shares appear to be premium-only.
- One search summary said 9.9% of 2025 releases earned $50k or more. That is hard to reconcile with 8% earning over $100k (it would leave only ~2% between $50k and $100k). The source was unclear, so I excluded the figure.
- No reliable 2024–2026 split of success rates between indie and non-indie titles.
- I could not open any page directly, so figures could not be checked against the original articles or reports.

## 2. Gross-to-net: what $300k gross is worth to the developer, and what gross produces $300k net

### Takeaway
A self-published developer keeps roughly **52–58% of Steam gross before income tax**. The deductions are refunds and chargebacks (~9–12%), VAT or sales tax (~9–15% of what remains) and Valve's 30%. So **$300k gross ≈ $157k–$173k pre-tax**. With a publisher revenue share that drops to **≈$79k–$121k**, and after an *illustrative* 25–35% income tax the self-published figure is **≈$102k–$130k**. **$300k net (pre-tax, self-published) needs ≈$520k–$575k gross.** It needs about $1.04M–$1.15M with a 50/50 publisher split, and up to ~$1.5M–$1.65M if income tax is also taken into account. Valve's lower 25% and 20% tiers only begin at $10M and $50M, so they don't matter at this scale.

### Cited Findings
- **Valve's revenue-share tiers.**
  - Announced 30 Nov 2018 and applied to revenue from 1 Oct 2018 onward: 30% up to $10M, 25% between $10M and $50M, 20% above $50M.
  - The count includes game packages, DLC, in-game sales and Community Market game fees.
  - Sources: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1753729); [Neowin](https://www.neowin.net/news/valve-introduces-new-revenue-share-tiers-for-steam-promising-bigger-cuts-for-studios/); [MCV/Develop](https://www.mcvuk.com/business-news/valves-new-steam-distribution-agreement-lets-big-game-devs-keep-more-of-their-profits/)
  - **Conflict on mechanics:** TechCrunch describes marginal brackets, while Neowin says the lower rate applies to all further sales once a threshold is crossed. 2026 third-party guides still describe the same three tiers — [Steam Page Analyzer (2026)](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-revenue-share-explained); [Fungies (2026)](https://fungies.io/steam-revenue-share-explained/)
- **How the thresholds are counted:** on adjusted gross revenue (after VAT, refunds and chargebacks) from direct Steam sales only. Keys sold through other stores don't count — [Fungies](https://fungies.io/steam-revenue-share-explained/) (third-party guide)
- **VAT and sales tax handling.**
  - Prices in the EU, UK, Australia and elsewhere include VAT or GST. Valve collects and remits it, and the developer's 70% is calculated on the price without tax. US sales tax is added on top at checkout — [Presskit.gg](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/how-much-does-steam-take)
  - Steam's commission is calculated on net revenue, which is gross minus adjustments (refunds, chargebacks, taxes). The gross figure includes tax collected at checkout — [Beancount bookkeeping guide, 9 Jul 2026](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/09/steam-revenue-share-indie-game-developer-bookkeeping-guide)
- **GameDiscoverCo's "getting to net" breakdown** (2020 data, older):
  - The waterfall: $1M gross → 9% refunds and chargebacks (by dollar value) → 91% → VAT and sales tax → 82.5% → Valve's 30% → **57.75% ($577,500)**.
  - It is cross-checked against a developer report of about 55% net.
  - Refund rates vary by country, with China notably higher than the US.

  Sources: [Game Developer repost](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/game-refunds-the-hidden-costs-of-getting-to-net-on-steam-2); [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/game-refunds-and-the-hidden-costs); [Game World Observer, 18 Aug 2020](https://gameworldobserver.com/2020/08/18/steam-devs-net-revenue). A reader comment disputes counting sales tax as a cost, since the customer pays it — [GameDiscoverCo comments](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/game-refunds-and-the-hidden-costs/comment/423726)
- **Refund rates.**
  - **GameDiscoverCo developer survey** (~150 developers; one summary dates it 2024, another 2025): median 9.5% and average 10.8% of *copies*. Early Access median 12.4%. Games averaging over 50 hours of playtime 7.4%; games with short average playtime 12.6% — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/steam-refunds-how-many-should-you); [GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gamediscoverco-steam-refunds-research)
  - **Other estimates:** "around 10%–12%" ([XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/steams-two-hour-refund-window-killing-niche-indie-games/), no methodology). A 10–15% median, and 18–28% for short narrative games ([Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-refund-rates-data), no sample size).
- **2026 worked example from a third-party guide:**
  - A $20 sale loses a ~15% VAT blend ($17), then 12% refunds (~$15), then Valve's 30% (~$10.50). That leaves about 52% of list price.
  - The same guide says "most developers keep around 40–55% of the US list price per unit, not 70%" — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-revenue-share-explained) / [Fungies](https://fungies.io/steam-revenue-share-explained/) (attribution between these two unverified)
  - Another guide puts the range at "you keep 35–57%" — [Immutable](https://www.immutable.com/guides/how-much-does-steam-take)
- **Regional pricing.**
  - Valve publishes no figure for the regional pricing haircut. Third-party estimates put the average price actually paid per unit 15–31% below the US price.
  - Weighting the regional tiers equally gives ~69% of the US price. But 50–60% of a typical indie game's revenue comes from the US and Western Europe, where prices are 90–105% of base, so the real haircut is smaller.
  - One anecdote: a $15 game averaged about $11.20 per sale.

  Sources: [Steam Page Analyzer regional guide](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-regional-pricing-guide); [Steam Page Analyzer fee calculator](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/tools/steam-fee-calculator); [steam-revenue-calculator.com](https://steam-revenue-calculator.com/blog/the-real-talk-on-steams-cut-what-your-game-actually-makes-and-why-its-complicated) (which figure comes from which page is unverified)
- **Discounts.**
  - Valve's cut applies to the discounted price. One guide says games that take part in sales regularly carry a lifetime average discount of 15–25% — [gtstu](https://gtstu.com/steam-indie-game-pricing-strategy/) / [SyncGTM](https://syncgtm.com/blog/do-steam-sales-harm-the-developers) (attribution unverified)
  - Older anecdote (January 2019): Jake Birkett ran a week-long sale with the same discounts as the previous July and earned 31% of that earlier sale's revenue, after Valve's October 2018 algorithm change — [Jake Birkett on X](https://x.com/GreyAlien/status/1086232780990746624)
- **Valve's $100k figure is gross:** "$100,000 ... instantly becomes $70,000 after Valve's 30% cut" — [IndieGames.eu](https://www.indie-games.eu/valve-shares-new-steam-stats-at-gdc-over-5800-games-earned-100k-on-steam-in-2025/)
- **Mike Rose:** after Valve's cut and taxes, a developer keeps about $50,000 of $100,000, which he says is too little to sustain a multi-person team — [17173 (secondary)](https://news.17173.com/content/03172026/140211422.shtml)
- **Publisher splits.** I found no authoritative dataset; guides describe a two-phase structure.
  - **During recoupment:** the publisher keeps 70–100% of net receipts until its advance is repaid.
  - **After recoupment:** typically 50/50 to 60/40 in the developer's favour, with some deals at 70/30 for the developer.
  - **Sliding-scale example:** 60/40 up to $1M net, 70/30 from $1M to $5M, 80/20 above $5M.
  - One guide says most games never earn back their advance.

  Sources: [Sanlo](https://www.sanlo.io/resources/what-to-know-about-game-publishing-deals); [Legal Moves (2026)](https://legalmoveslawfirm.com/video-game-royalties/); [PracticalMedia](https://practicalmedia.io/article/what-revenue-share-models-work-best-for-indie-game-publishing); [salivity](https://salivity.github.io/game-development/article/indie-game-publisher-negotiation-recoupment-and-mgs); [GameDiscoverCo on publishing contracts](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/what-makes-for-a-good-game-publishing)
- **Publisher-backed versus self-published:** in 2024, publisher-backed titles had a median about 5 times higher than self-published ones ($16,222 vs $3,285, among titles with at least $500 revenue). Selection bias applies, because publishers pick stronger projects — [OP Game Marketing](https://opgamemarketing.substack.com/p/the-2024-indie-and-aa-game-market) (attribution unverified)
- **Small-scale reality check:** Extraneum sold ~2,400 units for about $12,000 net after Steam's cut, taxes and refunds, roughly $5 per unit — [itch.io postmortem](https://davidjalbert.itch.io/extraneum/devlog/1090186/extraneum-post-mortem-one-year-later)

### Inferences
- **Derived: $300k gross waterfall** (self-published, using GameDiscoverCo's 2020 ratios):

| Step | Deduction | Remaining |
|---|---|---|
| Start | — | $300,000 |
| Refunds and chargebacks | −9% | $273,000 |
| VAT and sales tax | ≈ −9.3% of the remainder (91% → 82.5%) | $247,500 |
| Valve's cut | −30% | **$173,250 (57.75%)** |

  The 2026 guide's ratios give 0.85 × 0.88 × 0.70 = 52.4%, or **$157,080**. Mike Rose's rule of thumb (~50% after "cut and taxes") gives ~$150k.
- **Derived: after a publisher split** (assuming the advance is already recouped):
  - 50/50 split: $78.5k–$86.6k to the developer.
  - 70/30 in the developer's favour: $110k–$121k.
- **Derived: after income tax** (illustrative assumption only; rates depend on country and legal structure, and none were sourced): a 25–35% effective rate leaves **$102k–$130k** for a self-published game.
- **Derived: gross needed for $300k net.**

| Scenario | Gross needed |
|---|---|
| Pre-tax, self-published | 300k / 0.5775 = **$519k** to 300k / 0.5236 = **$573k** |
| After 25–35% tax, self-published | $693k–$881k |
| Pre-tax, 50/50 publisher split | $1.04M–$1.15M |
| 50/50 publisher split and 30% tax | $1.48M–$1.64M |

- **Derived: what $300k gross pays for.** $157k–$173k pre-tax covers about 3.1–3.5 person-years at an illustrative $50k all-in cost per person-year. A 5-person team working 2 years (10 person-years at $50k) would need ≈$866k–$955k gross just to break even.
- **Definitions of "gross" differ, and this is a material uncertainty.**
  - Valve's figures appear to be gross before its cut.
  - Third-party trackers estimate units × an estimated realized price.
  - Steamworks gross includes VAT.

  If a tracker's "$300k" already nets out regional pricing and discounts, the 52–58% ratio applies directly. If "$300k" means units × US list price, the developer's share could be ~30–40% lower again. The second figure is derived from the regional-pricing and discount ranges above.

### Gaps
- **Engine royalties:** I could not verify current engine fees (for example, an Unreal royalty above a revenue threshold) within the search budget.
- **Income tax:** effective rates depend on jurisdiction, and I found no sourced figures. The 25–35% used above is purely illustrative.
- **Primary sources:** I did not retrieve Valve's current Steam Distribution Agreement text or its Steamworks definitions of gross and net.
- **Tracker definitions:** I did not confirm what Gamalytic and VG Insights include in "gross" (discounts, regional pricing, VAT, refunds).
- **Refund data:** the refund survey reports refunds by copies, not dollars, and its year (2024 or 2025) is reported inconsistently.

## 3. Review-count and wishlist benchmarks associated with ~$300k gross

### Takeaway
At typical $15–$20 prices, $300k gross means roughly 15k–36k units. At 30–58 sales per review, that implies **roughly 260–1,190 Steam reviews, centred around 500–900**. This matches Zukowski's "Gold" tier ($250k–$999k), described as "probably 500–1,000+ reviews". For wishlists, GameDiscoverCo's latest medians give these derived targets:

- **Current-median case** (0.10 week-1 sales per launch wishlist for games over $10, and year one ≈ 2.46× week one): **~76k–113k launch wishlists for $300k in year one at $15–$20**.
- **Optimistic case** (0.20 and 3.16×): **~30k–44k**, which fits third-party guidance that 30k–50k wishlists is "Gold territory".

Revenue after year one lowers these targets. The widely cited ~7,000 wishlists is a visibility threshold (Steam's Popular Upcoming list), not a $300k target.

### Cited Findings
**Reviews-to-sales multipliers (the Boxleiter method and GameDiscoverCo's "NB number")**
- **GameDiscoverCo 2020 study** (237 games; older data):
  - Median 58 sales per review, average 63; the middle 80% fall between about 25 and 100.
  - Median by launch year: 74 before 2017, 51 in 2019, 38 in 2020. The drop is attributed to Valve's October 2019 review prompt.
  - Suggested planning range: 20–60.

  Sources: [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-that-game-sold-on-steam-using); [Steam Page Analyzer summary](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-review-to-sales-multiplier)
- **VG Insights** (over 10,000 games): games from 2020 cluster around 30x; games from 2014 and earlier run 70x or more — [Steam Page Analyzer, Boxleiter explainer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/boxleiter-method-explained) (attribution unverified)
- **Gamalytic:**
  - Games above 90% positive run about 30 sales per review; games near 70% positive run closer to 60x.
  - Free games get about twice the sales per review of paid games.
  - The ratio falls slightly every year, and genre "does not seem to be as important a factor".

  Sources: [Gamalytic deep dive](https://gamalytic.com/blog/a-deep-dive-into-the-steam-review-ratio); [Gamalytic estimation guide](https://gamalytic.com/blog/how-to-accurately-estimate-steam-sales)
- **We Love It** uses 35 sales per review, with 20 and 50 as bounds — [We Love It](https://weloveit.io/developers/steam-revenue-report/)
- **Steam Page Analyzer** uses 30x (20–40x) for 2026 releases. Its genre bands (Horror 30–50x, Simulation 25–40x) are its own assumptions; it says "no published study breaks the multiplier out by genre at that resolution" — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-review-to-sales-multiplier)
- **Unverified claim:** one calculator site says the NB number "averaged approximately 63 in 2025". This may be confused with the 2020 average of 63 — [Profitable](https://profitable.app/tools/steam-revenue-calculator)

**Converting wishlists to sales**
- **GameDiscoverCo, "State of Steam wishlist conversions 2024–2025"** (games launched with over 25,000 wishlists between about Sep 2024 and Aug/Sep 2025; sources give slightly different end dates):
  - Median 0.15 first-week sales per launch wishlist (about 15k sales per 100k wishlists).
  - Median 0.10 for games priced above $10 at launch, and about 0.14 excluding adult (NSFW) titles.
  - Individual results range from 10 times below the median to 20 times above it.

  Sources: [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/the-state-of-steam-wishlist-conversions); [GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gamediscoverco-the-state-of-steam); [GameDev Producer](https://gamedevproducer.com/steam-wishlist-to-sales/)
- **Earlier GameDiscoverCo figures:** a 2024 survey found games launching with over 10k wishlists had a median of 17% of their wishlist balance converted by the end of week one. An analysis of ~700 games (September 2023) found 22%. The samples differ, so the figures are not directly comparable — [GameDiscoverCo 2024](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/revealed-the-state-of-steam-wishlist); [GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gamediscoverco-conversion-benchmarks)
- **Year-one revenue multipliers:**
  - For all 2023 Steam releases, the median ratio of year-one to week-one revenue was 2.64x. It was 2.69x for games selling over 1,000 copies in week one, where the average was 4.22x.
  - An April 2024 survey gave a median of 3.16x. The first week is "often 30–40% of the first year sales".
  - Month one versus week one: 1.39x (2024), down from ~1.5x (2021).

  Sources: [GameDiscoverCo long-tail deep dive](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/data-deep-dive-whats-the-long-tail); [GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gamediscoverco-games-long-tail-revenue); [GameDiscoverCo 2024](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/revealed-the-state-of-steam-wishlist)
  - A July 2026 GameDiscoverCo study reportedly puts the median at about 2.46x — [GameDev Producer (secondary)](https://gamedevproducer.com/steam-wishlist-to-sales/)
- **Older rule of thumb:** year one = 2–5 times week one — [impress.games](https://impress.games/blog/steam-benchmarks-and-estimates-analysis); [frgmnts.blog, citing Simon Carless (c. 2020)](https://frgmnts.blog/f/how-make-successful-indie-game.html)

**Zukowski's tiers (How To Market A Game) and related guidance**
- **Revenue tiers** (lifetime gross): Bronze $0–10k ("algorithm ignores you"), Silver $10k–$249k, Gold $250k–$999k ("Valve actively promotes you"), Diamond $1M+ — [Presskit.gg](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/how-many-wishlists-to-launch); [Zukowski benchmarks page](https://howtomarketagame.com/benchmarks/) (I could not open this page)

- **The 7,000 figure:** roughly the number of wishlists needed to appear on Steam's Popular Upcoming list. It predates a reported June 2026 store refresh that may raise the effective bar. Many games launch with 7,000 wishlists and still land in Silver — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/how-many-wishlists-before-launch); [Presskit.gg](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/how-many-wishlists-to-launch)
- **Launch-wishlist tiers (sources conflict):**
  - A June 2026 update reportedly sets Bronze 5k, Silver 8k, Gold 50k, Diamond 90k — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/how-many-wishlists-before-launch); [GitHub issue citing Zukowski](https://github.com/JamesFlames/EldritchDynasty/issues/73)
  - An earlier version: Silver 6k, Gold 30k, Diamond 150k — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/how-to-get-steam-wishlists)

- **Steam Next Fest tiers** (wishlists gained during the festival): Bronze 0–999, Silver 1,000–6,999, Gold 7,000–9,999, Diamond 10,000+ — [Presskit.gg](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/analyzing-next-fest-results)
- **Median conversion by launch-wishlist bucket** (conversion period not stated): under 5k ~15%; 5k–39,999 ~20%; 40k–99,999 ~23%; 100k or more ~25% — [Presskit.gg](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/how-many-wishlists-to-launch)
- **Jake Birkett:** one week after launch, Forbidden Solitaire beat his "medium target" with a "Birkett ratio" of 0.23. I could not verify how the ratio is defined; it appears to be week-one sales relative to wishlists — [Jake Birkett on X](https://x.com/GreyAlien/status/2052457851663130995)
- **Older data (2021):** Zukowski's benchmarks for wishlists gained in the first two weeks of a Coming Soon page ranged from tens (Bronze) to a few thousand (Diamond) — [How To Market A Game (2021)](https://howtomarketagame.com/2021/12/12/how-many-wishlists-can-you-expect-when-you-launch-your-games-coming-soon-steam-page/)

### Inferences
- **Derived: reviews implied by $300k gross.**
  - **Units:** $300k ÷ (list price × a realized-price factor). The factor is 1.00 at full list price; 0.72 assumes 15% regional and 15% average-discount haircuts; 0.56 assumes 25% and 25%.
  - **Reviews:** units ÷ the sales-per-review multiplier.

| List price | Units (1.00 / 0.72 / 0.56) | Reviews at 30x | Reviews at 58x | Reviews at 20x |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $4.99 | 60k / 83k / 107k | 2,004–3,563 | 1,037–1,843 | 3,006–5,344 |
| $9.99 | 30k / 42k / 53k | 1,001–1,780 | 518–920 | 1,502–2,669 |
| $14.99 | 20k / 28k / 36k | 667–1,186 | 345–613 | 1,001–1,779 |
| $19.99 | 15k / 21k / 27k | 500–889 | 259–460 | 750–1,334 |
| $24.99 | 12k / 17k / 21k | 400–711 | 207–368 | 600–1,067 |

  For $15–$20 games this agrees with Zukowski's "500–1,000+ reviews" for Gold. A $4.99 game needs about 2,000–3,500 reviews at 30x.
- **Derived: launch wishlists needed for $300k in year one.**
  - **Formula:** Week-1 gross needed = $300k ÷ (year-one multiplier). Week-1 units = week-1 gross ÷ (list price × 0.72–0.80). Wishlists = week-1 units ÷ conversion.
  - **Assumption:** a launch realized-price factor of 0.72 (10% launch discount and 20% regional haircut) to 0.80 (no launch discount). This is my assumption, not a sourced figure.

| Year-1 ÷ week-1 | List price | WL needed at 0.10 | WL at 0.15 | WL at 0.20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.46x (Jul 2026 study) | $9.99 | 153k–170k | 102k–113k | 76k–85k |
| 2.46x | $14.99 | 102k–113k | 68k–75k | 51k–56k |
| 2.46x | $19.99 | 76k–85k | 51k–56k | 38k–42k |
| 2.46x | $24.99 | 61k–68k | 41k–45k | 31k–34k |
| 3.16x (Apr 2024 survey) | $14.99 | 79k–88k | 53k–59k | 40k–44k |
| 3.16x | $19.99 | 59k–66k | 40k–44k | 30k–33k |

  - **Why the targets are uncertain:** These are year-one targets; revenue after year one would lower them, but I found no sourced multiplier beyond year one. Also, GameDiscoverCo's 0.10–0.15 medians come from games with over 25k wishlists, a selected and generally more visible group. Smaller launches may convert at different rates, and Presskit.gg's bucket table suggests ~15–20% conversion under 40k wishlists.
- **Derived: the ~7,000-wishlist visibility threshold.** At the 0.10–0.20 conversion rates above, 7,000 launch wishlists maps to roughly 700–1,400 week-one units. Under the same multipliers that is about $19k–$59k in year one at $15–$20, or up to ~$71k with the 3.16x multiplier. Calculation: 700 × $14.99 × 0.72 × 2.46 ≈ $18.6k; 1,400 × $19.99 × 0.80 × 2.64 ≈ $59.1k. That puts it in Zukowski's Silver band.
- **Implication:** a launch-wishlist count in the tens of thousands, roughly 30k–110k depending on price and conversion, is the typical precondition for a $300k year one. That level is far beyond what most Steam games reach, which fits the 3–4% base rate in Section 1.

### Gaps
- I found no dataset that directly reports the review counts or launch wishlists of games that reached $300k.
- No published study gives review multipliers by genre; Gamalytic says genre is not a major factor.
- I found no sourced multiplier for revenue after year one (lifetime ÷ year one).
- I could not open Zukowski's own benchmarks page, so the conflicting launch-wishlist tiers could not be resolved. The effect of the reported June 2026 Steam store refresh is unknown.
- One headline says "Only 10% of new Steam games have wishlist-to-sales conversion rate higher than 1.9%" ([Game World Observer, Dec 2024](https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/12/06/wishlist-to-sales-ratio-steam-gamediscoverco-benchmark)). I did not retrieve how that metric is defined, and it appears to measure something other than week-one sales per wishlist.

## 4. Typical price points by genre and units needed for $300k

### Takeaway
Among commercially successful Steam games, indie prices cluster at **$9.99–$24.99, with $19.99 then $14.99 the most common**. The median launch price of top-grossing new releases fell from **~$19.50 (early 2023) to ~$15.64 (late 2025)**. **$300k gross needs about 15,000 units at $19.99 list price, 20,000 at $14.99, 30,000 at $9.99 and 60,000 at $4.99.** Allowing for regional pricing and lifetime discounting raises that to about 1.4–1.8 times those numbers. I found no reliable 2024–2026 dataset of median price by genre; genre guidance is qualitative.

### Cited Findings
- **AppMagic** (top 1,000 Steam games):
  - Most common price points: $19.99 (147 games), $14.99 (124), $9.99 and $24.99 (92 each).
  - Price tiers: indie mostly up to $29.99; AA from $30 to $59; AAA $59.99–$69.99.
  - "You're unlikely to find a Clicker or an Auto Battler sold for $24.99". Deeper strategic tags such as Diplomacy or MOBA often land in the $20–$25 range.

  Source: [AppMagic Steam Pricing Research](https://appmagic.rocks/research/steam-pricing-research)
- **Median launch price trend:** the median launch price of the top 50 new releases by revenue fell from ~$19.50 (early 2023) to ~$15.64 (late 2025). The same guide says "viral, multiplayer-first concepts can thrive at $7.99–$9.99, while single-player narrative, action-adventure, and simulation games with strong production values continue to perform at $14.99–$19.99". It does not name its dataset — [gtstu (2026 guide)](https://gtstu.com/steam-indie-game-pricing-strategy/)
- **2025 hits at low prices (outliers):** PEAK at $7.99 sold ~15.7M units for ~$92.3M; R.E.P.O. at $9.99 sold ~18.4M units for ~$145.8M; Hollow Knight: Silksong at ~$20 made ~$81.6M — [Medium analysis](https://medium.com/@research_86150/steam-in-2025-the-year-indie-games-ate-the-industry-alive-666abf04551f) (secondary estimates)
- **Lifetime discounting:** games that take part in sales regularly carry a lifetime average discount of 15–25% (unverified guide figure) — [gtstu](https://gtstu.com/steam-indie-game-pricing-strategy/)
- **Regional pricing:** the average price paid is ~15–31% below the US price (third-party estimates; see Section 2) — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-regional-pricing-guide)
- **Copies benchmark:** only 1.8% of 2025 releases exceeded 100,000 copies — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/analysts-report-drop-in-game-revenue-on-steam-despite-growing-number-of-releases)

### Inferences

| $4.99 | 20,041 / 27,738 / 35,627 | **60,121 / 83,212 / 106,881** | 200,401 / 277,372 / 356,269 |
| $7.99 | 12,516 / 17,323 / 22,251 | **37,547 / 51,969 / 66,751** | 125,157 / 173,227 / 222,501 |
| $9.99 | 10,011 / 13,855 / 17,796 | **30,031 / 41,565 / 53,387** | 100,101 / 138,547 / 177,956 |
| $14.99 | 6,672 / 9,234 / 11,860 | **20,014 / 27,701 / 35,580** | 66,712 / 92,334 / 118,598 |
| $19.99 | 5,003 / 6,924 / 8,894 | **15,008 / 20,772 / 26,681** | 50,026 / 69,239 / 88,934 |
| $24.99 | 4,002 / 5,539 / 7,114 | **12,005 / 16,616 / 21,342** | 40,017 / 55,386 / 71,140 |
| $29.99 | 3,335 / 4,616 / 5,928 | **10,004 / 13,846 / 17,784** | 33,345 / 46,152 / 59,280 |

  Worked example: $300,000 ÷ ($14.99 × 0.7225) = 300,000 ÷ 10.83 ≈ 27,700 units.
- **Low prices need very high volume.** A game priced at $2.99–$4.99 needs roughly 60k–180k units to reach $300k. Only 1.8% of 2025 releases passed 100k copies, so very low prices make $300k depend on viral volume. Higher prices ($19.99–$24.99) need far fewer units, but AppMagic and gtstu indicate those prices are associated with genres (strategy, simulation, narrative) and production values that players expect to justify them.
- **Genre and price (qualitative, from the sources above):**
  - Clickers, idle and auto-battler games: rarely priced at $24.99 (AppMagic).

  - Strategy and diplomacy: often $20–$25 (AppMagic).

### Gaps

- The realized-price factor (regional mix plus lifetime discounts) has no Valve figure. The 0.56–0.72 range is built from unverified third-party estimates.
- One search summary claimed "median revenue" of $150k–$400k for colony and management sims and $100k–$300k for roguelites (source likely Steam Page Analyzer; attribution unverified). It conflicts by orders of magnitude with the all-game medians in Section 1 and is probably conditioned on successful titles, so I excluded it from the findings.

## 5. Budgets, team sizes and timelines by genre; what 1–5 person teams can realistically do; small-team examples at $300k–$5M

### Takeaway
Hard evidence is thin. Indie budget figures by genre come from vendor and blog estimates, not audited data:

- **Solo:** $10k–$50k.
- **2–5 person teams:** $50k–$300k over 12–18 months.
- **By genre:** roguelites $40k–$200k; 2D RPGs $50k–$250k; factory and automation games $80k–$300k; 3D RPGs $200k–$1M+; multiplayer games $100k–$500k over 12–24 months.

VG Insights' 2024 segmentation pegs 1–2 person "hobby" developers at ~2k–20k copies and ~$50k revenue, and 3–15 person "small teams" at ~20k–200k copies and ~$1M. **$300k sits between those two benchmarks.** It is a strong result for a solo or duo team with a low monthly cost (burn rate), but below what a typical 3–5 person team needs to break even. Two caveats on genre feasibility:

- **Genres within reach of a $300k outcome:** 2D, single-player, systems-driven genres have budget ranges that fit inside what a $300k outcome nets the developer.
- **High-cost genres:** 3D RPGs and multiplayer games have budget floors at or above that net. The sources I found give no indie-scale budgets for open-world RPGs, multiplayer shooters or MMOs.

**I could not compile a verified set of 2024–2026 small-team games earning $300k–$5M with team size and development time; the search budget ran out.** The examples found are older, extreme outliers, or typical failures.

- **2025 small-studio hits were extreme outliers:** Schedule I ~$151M, R.E.P.O. ~$146–147M, PEAK ~$92M. Team sizes were not verified in this session — [Medium analysis](https://medium.com/@research_86150/steam-in-2025-the-year-indie-games-ate-the-industry-alive-666abf04551f)
- **Discounty:** a simulation game that sold 100k copies in its first week (GameDiscoverCo case study). I did not retrieve its price or team size — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-discounty-rang-up-100k-sales)

### Inferences
- **Derived: what $300k gross supports.**
  - **Solo:** $157k–$173k pre-tax (Section 2) covers about 3–3.5 person-years at an illustrative $50k all-in cost. That makes $300k a sustainable result for a solo developer working 2–3 years, or a duo working about 1.5 years.
  - **5 people for 2 years:** needs ≈$866k–$955k gross just to break even at $50k per person-year. That is close to VG Insights' ~$1M "Small Teams" benchmark.
- **Genre feasibility for 1–5 person teams** (inference from the budget ranges, price expectations and VG Insights tiers, not from direct evidence):
  - **Likely feasible:** genres whose estimated indie budgets fall well below ~$157k–$173k net, including 2D puzzle and casual, 2D platformer, roguelite and deckbuilder, idle and incremental, short horror, and 2D management or simulation.
  - **High-cost:** 3D RPG ($200k–$1M+) and multiplayer ($100k–$500k, plus server and live-ops costs) can consume all of a $300k outcome. Open-world RPGs, multiplayer shooters and MMOs, for which I found no indie-scale budgets, would sit higher still.
  - **Caveat:** this inference rests on vendor estimates of low reliability.
- **Benchmarks for small teams:** VG Insights' "Small Teams" benchmark (~$1M; 20k–200k copies) suggests $300k is a below-typical result for a professionalised 3–15 person team. Its "Hobby" benchmark (~$50k) suggests $300k is about six times typical for 1–2 people.
- **Survivorship bias:** postmortems and "famous budget" lists over-represent successes. The Extraneum case (~$12k net after ~3 years) is closer to the median outcome in Section 1.

### Gaps
- **Missing deliverable:** I could not compile verified 2024–2026 examples of small teams earning $300k–$5M with team size, development time and revenue all sourced; the search budget ran out. **Leads for follow-up (no figures asserted or verified):** Buckshot Roulette, Iron Lung, Mouthwashing, Crow Country, Luck be a Landlord, Cobalt Core, Shogun Showdown, Rusty's Retirement, Nodebuster, Dome Keeper, Thronefall, Discounty. ## 6. Genre-specific risk factors

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced risks are:

1. **Refunds.** Short games and Early Access titles see higher refund rates (12.6% and 12.4% medians) than games averaging over 50 hours of playtime (7.4%). Outliers such as Paddle Paddle Paddle reached 21%.
2. **Launch concentration.** Week one is often 30–40% of year-one sales, and most Early Access games earn less at their 1.0 launch than at their Early Access launch.
3. **Price and production-value expectations by genre.** Cheap, viral co-op games sell at $8–$10; strategy, simulation and narrative games with strong production values sell at $15–$25.
4. **Multiplayer and co-op are hit-driven.** Games with co-op modes grossed $8.2B on Steam in 2025, but revenue concentration is extreme (the top 1% of games take 84.5% of revenue), and budgets are higher.

I found no quantified data on concurrent-player thresholds for multiplayer viability, live-ops costs, content-hour expectations, or the revenue effect of art and production values.

### Inferences
- **Short, story-driven genres** (short horror, narrative, "walking sim"): cheaper to make, but more exposed to the 2-hour refund window. The evidence ranges from 12.6% (survey, short playtime) to 21% (Paddle Paddle Paddle), against 7.4% for long-playtime games. Content that runs well past 2 hours, or that players replay, lowers the risk.
- **Replayable systems genres** (roguelite, deckbuilder, idle, management): long average playtimes are associated with the lowest refund rates (7.4%). AppMagic notes idle, clicker and auto-battler games are rarely priced at $24.99, so they tend to need more units per dollar.
- **Multiplayer and co-op:** the large aggregate revenue ($8.2B) sits alongside extreme hit concentration. The sources say nothing about the risk of a game emptying out without enough concurrent players, or about live-ops costs, so these risks remain unquantified, not disproven.
- **Early Access:** because the launch spike is concentrated, an Early Access launch "spends" a game's biggest visibility moment, and 1.0 launches usually earn less. That is a structural risk for live-ops-style or content-heavy genres that rely on Early Access.
- **Content-hungry genres** (RPG and strategy): these tolerate higher prices ($20–$25 for strategy tags). The budget estimates for 3D RPGs ($200k–$1M+) suggest the content-hour expectations carry over into higher costs, but I found no direct content-hour data.

### Gaps
- I found no data on the concurrent-player counts multiplayer games need to stay viable, or on how often indie multiplayer games fail because they never reach them.
- I found no figures on live-ops cost burden (servers, content cadence) for small teams.
- I found no quantified evidence on content-hour expectations by genre (RPG or strategy) or on how art and production values affect revenue.
- I found no genre-level refund rates beyond the playtime buckets. Genre-level success rates are covered by the parallel research on revenue by genre, not here.
