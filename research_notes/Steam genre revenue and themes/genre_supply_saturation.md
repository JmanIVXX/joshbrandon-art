## 1. How many games were released on Steam each year from 2020 to 2025 and in 2026 year-to-date, and what share of releases carries each major genre/tag?

### Takeaway
Annual Steam game releases roughly doubled, from about 9.6k in 2020 to about 19k–21k in 2025 depending on the tracker. The biggest jump came in 2024 (+31–36%), and growth slowed in 2025 (+8% by SteamDB, +20% by Indie Launch Lab). In 2026, releases are running at about 70+ per day: roughly 20k by 1 Oct 2026, on course for a third consecutive record of roughly 25k–28k (my extrapolation).

In 2025, about 72% of releases carried Steam's "Indie" genre, 47% "Casual", 39% "Adventure", 37% "Action" and 25% "Simulation". Games carry several labels, so these shares add up to more than 100%.

### Inferences
- **"Indie" and "Casual" say little about saturation.** At ~72% and ~47% of releases they are near-default labels, not competitive niches. The competitive sets that matter are narrower sub-genre tag combinations.
- **Every other genre is below Simulation's share.** Simulation (5,217) is the fifth-largest broad genre in 2025, so Strategy, RPG, Sports, Racing and the rest each carried fewer than ~24.5% of 2025 releases.
- **Hit rates have fallen.** Releases doubled from 2020 to 2025, while 2025 produced only 608 games with 1,000+ reviews. The probability that any single release becomes a hit has therefore fallen. This is directional only: a matching 2020 hit count was not retrieved.
- **2026 extrapolations (mine, not published).**
  - SteamDB: 16,115 by day 232 (~69.5 per day) projects to ~25.4k for the year.
  - Games-Stats: 20,220 by day 273 (~74 per day) projects to ~27k.
  - Either way, 2026 should finish about 20–30% above 2025. That would be a re-acceleration after 2025's slowdown.
- **Roguelites are a rising share of supply.** The tag grew faster in 2025 (+28%) than total releases (+8% SteamDB, +20% Indie Launch Lab).

## 2. Which genres/tags are released the most by volume, and which are growing fastest or shrinking (absolute and share)?

### Takeaway
By volume, the broad genres rank Indie > Casual > Adventure > Action > Simulation (2025). Among narrower niches, the largest are sexual content (~9% of releases), roguelites (~9%) and horror (~6%).

Documented supply-side growth:
- Roguelites: +28% in 2025.
- Co-op: the share of descriptions mentioning co-op rose 1.4% → 2.1% from 2023 to 2025.
- Cozy farming sims: roughly doubled year-on-year in early 2026 (small sample).
- Survivors-likes: Valve formalised the "Bullet Heaven" tag in May 2026.

Sexual content is shrinking as a share of releases, and horror lost its #1 position by hit count in 2025. On the demand side, Steam playtime grew fastest in Action (+28%) and Strategy (+24%) in H1 2026, while RPG revenue declined in 2025. A clean 2020→2026 release series by tag could not be obtained.

### Inferences
- **Supply follows breakouts with a lag.** The documented supply growth clusters in genres that produced breakout hits in 2022–2025:
  - roguelites and deckbuilders after Balatro;
  - co-op and friendslop after Lethal Company and R.E.P.O.;
  - cozy farming sims;
  - survivors-likes after Vampire Survivors.

  That is consistent with a copycat lag of 1–3 years.
- **Possible lifecycle timing (my inference).** If tag trends surge for about four years (arXiv), survivors-likes (2022 origin) would be near the end of their surge in 2026, and friendslop (late-2023 origin) mid-surge. The paper does not say this; it is my extrapolation from its average.
- **Strategy is the clearest demand-growth case without matching supply evidence** (+24% playtime). No strategy release-count series was found, so the case that strategy is under-supplied rests on demand and revenue data only (see Q3/Q4).

- No year-by-year (2020–2025) release counts were retrieved for Action, Adventure, Casual, Simulation, Strategy, RPG, Puzzle, Platformer, Visual Novel, Horror, Survival, Shooter, Sports or Racing. - No release counts by year were found for incremental/idle games, shop or job simulators, survivors-likes, colony sims or automation.

## 3. How does each genre's share of releases compare with its share of revenue, copies sold or player time? Published opportunity / competition / demand-vs-supply metrics

### Takeaway
Revenue is far more concentrated by genre than releases are.
- **Top-end revenue.** Among games that grossed over $1M, Action takes about 58% of lifetime Steam revenue, RPG 17%, Strategy 14%, Simulation 10% and Sports 1% (GameDiscoverCo). Sensor Tower puts Action at about 33% and Shooters at about 15% of 2025 Steam premium revenue.
- **Releases vs revenue weight.** Simulation (~25% of releases) and Casual (~47%) are far more common in releases than in revenue.
- **Hit rates favour low-supply genres.** On the share of releases that become hits, low-supply genres beat high-supply ones. In 2025, Open World Survival Craft (0.4% of releases) had a 20.8% hit rate against a 3.0% average; farming had 8.3% and roguelike deckbuilders 5.1%. Horror (3.2%) and sexual content (1.1%) were at or below average (Zukowski).
- **Sandbox leads the "bankability" study.** The Totally Human Media/VaporLens study ranks sandbox first: 27.6% of sandbox games reach $100k or more, and sandbox tops its "crowding vs payoff" measure for 2026.

- **How the estimators work.** Gamalytic, VG Insights, Alinea, GameDiscoverCo and Totally Human Media all model sales from public signals: mainly review counts, price and tags. ### Inferences
- **Release share vs revenue share.** This comparison is directional only, because the taxonomies differ: Sensor Tower lists "shooters" separately from "action", while Steam's Action genre includes shooters.
  - **Action:** about 37% of 2025 releases, against about 33% (or ~48% with shooters) of 2025 Steam premium revenue and 58% of $1M+ lifetime revenue. Its revenue is concentrated in big-budget and live-service titles.
  - **Simulation:** about 25% of releases but about 10% of $1M+ revenue, so it under-indexes at the top end. Zukowski's narrower Simulation/Management bucket nonetheless over-indexes on hits (5.2% of releases, 7.1% of hits).
  - **Casual:** about 47% of releases, with no distinct revenue presence in any report.
  - **Strategy:** about 14% of $1M+ revenue, with +24% playtime growth and Alinea's "performs better" note. Its share of releases is under 24.5% but otherwise unknown.
- **Opportunity ratios from Zukowski's 2025 table.** Hit rate divided by the 3.0% average (my calculation):
  - Open World Survival Craft about 7x, Farming about 2.8x, Roguelike Deckbuilder about 1.7x, Simulation/Management about 1.4x, Horror about 1.1x, Sexual content about 0.4x.
  - Caveat: survival-craft and sandbox games are usually larger, costlier productions, and the OWSC sample is small (72 games). Part of the ratio therefore reflects production scope, not the genre alone.
- **Tie to the $300k "moderate success" bar (my inference, not a published figure).**
  - Three anchors bracket the success rate:
    - 9.9% of 2025 releases reached $50k or more within months (Gamalytic).
    - 19.5–27.6% of paid games in the best-performing genres reach $100k or more over their lifetime (Totally Human Media, all-time).
    - 3.0% of 2025 releases reached 1,000+ reviews in their launch year (Zukowski).
  - 1,000 reviews corresponds to tens of thousands of copies under commonly used review multipliers. Totally Human Media uses the Boxleiter method, but I did not verify the multiplier in this session. At typical indie prices, that puts the 1,000-review rate as a rough proxy for the share of releases near or above $300k gross.
  - On that basis, the base rate for $300k+ is probably low single-digit percent of all releases. In the best-performing niches it may be roughly 5–20%.
- **Revenue shares overstate indie room in Action and RPG.** The GameDiscoverCo, Sensor Tower and Totally Human Media revenue shares are dominated by large studios and evergreen titles. For judging saturation for non-AAA developers, hit-rate metrics are the better guide.

## 4. Which specific sub-genres show evidence of saturation by 2025–2026, and which appear under-supplied relative to player demand?

### Takeaway
- **Oversupply.** The evidence most consistently points to oversupply in five areas. The signal is high or rising volume with an at-or-below-average hit rate, or analysts calling a genre crowded:
  - sexual-content games;
  - horror as a broad category;
  - survivors-likes/bullet heavens;
  - action roguelites;
  - older evidence on platformers, FPS and Match-3; plus Souls-likes, which are AAA-dominated.
- **Under-supply.** The signal is low volume with high hit rates, or growing demand. The best-supported cases are open-world survival craft/sandbox and strategy (demand side only). Weaker cases are farming/cozy sims (where supply is surging), roguelike deckbuilders (busy but above-average hit rate), sports and co-op-only (non-friendslop) games.
- **Contested.** Friendslop is disputed. Zukowski argues it isn't saturated because the audience keeps growing and moves between games.
- **No data.** There is too little data to judge incremental games, shop/job sims, colony sims or automation.

- **Hit rate falls as volume rises.** In the 2025 data, low-volume, higher-scope genres beat high-volume, low-scope ones. Open-world survival craft, sandbox and farming outperform sexual content and horror. This fits a reading in which most of the supply surge comes from small, fast-to-make games concentrated in a few tags.
- **Friendslop shows release counts can mislead.** A genre with high churn and a growing audience can absorb many releases. Returns there are top-heavy and depend on timing.

### Gaps
- No release-count series was found for survivors-likes, deckbuilders, shop sims, incremental games, colony sims or automation.
- No data was found specifically on short horror (under 2 hours).
- The under-supply cases for strategy, colony sims and automation rest on demand-side signals. No published supply-vs-demand ratio was found for them.
- Estimators disagree on individual co-op hits. For example, AppMagic puts R.E.P.O. at 18.5M copies, while search extracts attribute a lower 16.4M to Alinea; the originating page was not confirmed.

## 5. What share of Steam releases are tagged Indie, and what share of Steam revenue/playtime goes to current-year releases vs back catalog/evergreen titles?

### Takeaway
- **Indie share of releases.** About 72% of 2025 releases carry Steam's Indie genre. VG Insights, by its own definition, classifies 99.9% of releases as indie.
- **Indie share of revenue is disputed.** VG Insights put it at 48% of full-game revenue as of Sep 2024; a 2025 analysis says about a quarter.
- **Playtime goes mostly to older games.** Valve's Steam Replay 2025 shows only 14% of 2025 playtime went to games released in 2025. Games 1–7 years old took 44%, and games 8+ years old 40%.
- **New releases are getting a falling share of revenue.** Alinea estimates that same-year releases took just 21% of Steam revenue in H1 2026, down from 27% (H1 2025) and 29% (H1 2024).

### Inferences
- **New releases are competing for a shrinking slice.** They get about 14% of playtime and about 21–29% of revenue. A rough illustration (my arithmetic, combining estimates from different sources):
  - H1 2026: $11.1B × 21% ≈ $2.3B went to 2026 releases. Spread over roughly 12–12.5k H1 releases (from SteamDB's ~70 per day), that is a mean of about $190k per release.
  - H1 2025: about $9.7B × 27% ≈ $2.6B over roughly 10k releases, a mean of about $260k.
  - That implies the mean revenue per new title fell about 25–30%. The median sits orders of magnitude below the mean (Gamalytic's 2025 median was $249).
- **Back-catalog incumbents count as competition too.** Where evergreen titles absorb most playtime, low release counts can still mean a crowded field. Examples:
  - shooters and MOBAs (Counter-Strike 2, Dota 2 and PUBG led Steam Replay's most-played list — [MP1st](https://mp1st.com/news/only-14-of-steam-playtime-went-to-2025-games));
  - survival-craft and sandbox games;
  - colony sims.
- **The indie revenue share depends on definition.** It ranges from about 25% to about 48% depending on whether self-published hits at AA/AAA scale are counted as indie (e.g. Black Myth: Wukong, per Sensor Tower's note). Any indie revenue-share figure should be quoted with its definition.

### Gaps
- No split of new-release vs back-catalog revenue by genre was found.
- Valve's Steam Replay does not break playtime down by genre.
- Newzoo and Ampere data on playtime by game age and genre for PC were not retrieved, because the search budget ran out.
- The share of each genre's 2025–2026 revenue going to current-year releases is unknown. It would show how crowded each genre really is, net of evergreen incumbents.
