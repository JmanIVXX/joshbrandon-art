# Which game genres publishers most actively sign and fund for PC/Steam (2024–2026)

> **Method and reliability note (read first).** I compiled these notes on 13 Oct 2026. WebFetch failed with a DNS error on every domain I tried: Substack, GameDiscoverCo, PC Gamer, Game Developer, How To Market A Game and Naavik. Direct downloads were also blocked by the network proxy (HTTP 403). After about 25 searches, the shared web-search budget for this run ran out. **Every finding below therefore comes from search-engine extracts of the cited pages, not from reading the full pages.** Treat exact figures as "as reported in the extract". Before publishing a load-bearing number, check it against the original page. Where an extract did not make clear which page a claim came from, I say so. I did not research about 20 of the 30 publishers named in the brief (see the Gaps under Q1).
>
> Labels used: **[HARD DATA]** means portfolio counts, financial filings, deal datasets or revenue estimates with a stated method. **[OPINION/ANECDOTE]** means interviews, commentary or blog advice. **[OLDER DATA]** means anything before 2024. **[AUTHOR CLASSIFICATION]** means a genre label I assigned from general knowledge of the title, which I could not verify in this session.

## Q1. What genres dominate the 2024–2026 release slates and announced signings of the most active indie/AA publishers on Steam?

### Takeaway
The 2024–2026 slates I could document lean towards **systems-driven, replayable designs**: strategy/management, roguelite or roguelike hybrids, RPGs and co-op/multiplayer. Hooded Horse is 100% strategy by policy. Devolver's 2025 hits were a roguelite and a roguelike deckbuilder, alongside a co-op shooter. Kepler is RPG-heavy. Narrative or art-led games are still funded, but mostly by curator-style labels such as Annapurna and by indie-funded vehicles such as Outersloth. Coverage is partial: I documented only 8 of the 30 named publishers or funds.

### Inferences
- In the slates I could document, **strategy/management, roguelite hybrids, RPGs and co-op/multiplayer** make up most identifiable 2024–2026 titles. Pure narrative games show up mainly at Annapurna and in the Outersloth fund. This rests on a small, non-random sample (8 entities), so it is suggestive rather than representative.
- Devolver's split (64% back catalogue) and its focus on premium titles with "long-term engagement" through paid and free updates point towards replayable, updatable games. A one-and-done linear game is harder to fit into that model. This is my interpretation of the company's own statements.
- Hooded Horse's strategy-only policy is a large, verifiable example of a publisher that is open about genre. A developer outside strategy/management would not fit its remit, even with its broad definition.

## Q2. What genres do publishers and their scouts explicitly say they are looking for or avoiding?

### Takeaway
Explicit, quotable 2024–2026 genre wish-lists from publishers were **scarce** in the sources I could retrieve. The clearest public genre policy is Hooded Horse's broadly defined strategy-only focus. What sources state most consistently is **not a genre but proof of demand and lower risk**: playable prototypes or demos, wishlists, community, proven franchises and long-tail or updatable potential. Older Steam data and recent commentary point to roguelikes and deckbuilders being both commercially strong and crowded, and to pure platformers being weak.

### Cited Findings
- **Hooded Horse:** strategy only, broadly defined ("it's not like we seal ourselves in"). Bender says a purely money-driven publisher would "ignore two thirds of your portfolio and focus on the third that is doing best", which he presents as a reason not to operate that way [OPINION, 2026]. — [Olden Era interview (Yahoo syndication)](https://currently.att.yahoo.com/att/heroes-might-magic-olden-era-160000824.html); [I.N.T.](https://int-magazine.com/interview/hooded-horse-founders-tim-bender-snow-rui/)
- A GDC talk titled **"25 Games Signed Without a Pitch Deck"** appeared in results for Hooded Horse queries. That suggests Hooded Horse signs from playable builds rather than decks, but **I could not confirm the speaker or content.** — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1034410/25-Games-Signed-Without-a)
- **Devolver (FY2025, Apr 2026):** the company is "prioritising premium titles with long-term engagement through meaningful paid for and free content updates" [company statement]. — [Devolver FY2025 announcement](https://investors.devolverdigital.com/files/downloads-and-publications/2026/devolver--full-year-2025-announcement.pdf)
- **GDC State of the Game Industry 2026** (Jan 2026; more than 2,300 respondents; ±3% margin; Omdia and Game Developer) [HARD DATA for the percentages, OPINION for the characterisations]:
  - Developers report publishers are "increasingly risk-averse, prioritizing proven franchises and predictable returns".
  - Many studios must show strong prototypes or demos before securing funding.
  - 74% of respondents work on premium (buy-to-play) games.
  - Sources: [GameDev Reports summary](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gdc-the-state-of-the-game-industry-fcf); [Esports Legal News, 6 Feb 2026](https://esportslegal.news/2026/02/06/gdc-state-of-the-game-industry-report/); [GDC SOTI 2026 registration page](https://reg.gdconf.com/2026-SOTI/)
- Publisher "identity" descriptions from a pitching guide [OPINION, low-rigour blog, date not confirmed]:
  - Devolver: "stylized, mechanically sharp" games.
  - Raw Fury: "personal, emotionally driven" projects.
  - Curve Games: works across genres but "leans toward charm over grit".
  - Square Enix Collective: open to many genres if the project is commercially promising.
  - Source: [Wayline blog](https://www.wayline.io/blog/pitch-indie-game-publishers-steam-wishlist-campaign)
- Chucklefish's pitching guide advises being signed about 6 months before launch [OPINION; date not confirmed, possibly older]. — [Chucklefish guide](https://chucklefish.org/blog/guide-for-pitching-to-publishers/)
- **Genre performance signals that publishers cite (older, but still widely referenced):**
  - [OLDER DATA, Apr 2022] Chris Zukowski found **roguelike deckbuilders had the best average sales** among indie genres on Steam, while NSFW was the tag with the most releases. — [Game World Observer, 22 Apr 2022](https://gameworldobserver.com/2022/04/22/roguelike-deckbuilders-beat-all-indie-genres-on-steam-in-terms-of-sales-but-only-99-such-titles-released-since-2019)
  - [OLDER/UNDATED] His analysis also concluded it is "hard for an indie developer to make a profitable platformer unless it is mixed with other genres like metroidvania or roguelike". — [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/stores-and-publishing/item-44107)
  - [OPINION, 2025–26] A marketing newsletter citing Zukowski says **212 roguelike deckbuilders shipped on Steam in 2025** and argues the field has hit "peak roguelite". — [Spawn Point Marketing](https://spawnpointmarketing.substack.com/p/roguelite-fatigue-is-brewing-on-the)

### Inferences
- The evidence points to publishers screening on **demonstrated demand and replay or long-tail potential** more than on a fixed genre list. Genres that naturally produce that evidence get through the filter more easily: strategy/management, roguelite hybrids, co-op and RPGs. These genres generate wishlists and demo engagement, and support DLC and updates. This is an inference; no single source states it as policy across publishers.
- Pure platformers and narrative-only games face a structural disadvantage under these criteria. That fits the older Zukowski data on platformers, but I found no 2024–2026 publisher statement explicitly saying "we avoid narrative" or "we avoid platformers". Annapurna and Outersloth still fund narrative games.
- Sources disagree on roguelikes: strong average sales (2022 data) versus saturation, with 212 roguelike deckbuilders in 2025.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025 or 2026 survey of publishers' genre preferences. The query returned book-publishing surveys, which are irrelevant.
- How To Market A Game posts on what publishers want could not be retrieved (DNS failure on WebFetch; the search did not surface them).
- No 2024–2026 interviews were retrieved with scouts or BD leads at Raw Fury, Team17, Playstack, Kepler, Fireshine and others.
- No source was found with explicit "avoid" lists (for example "no visual novels" or "no puzzle games").

## Q3. Is there data on the share of publisher-backed Steam releases by genre, and on success rates or median revenue of published versus self-published games by genre?

### Takeaway
There is hard data that **published games outperform self-published ones on average**. For 2024 releases, published games had about 2× the mean revenue and about 5× the median, and were about 4× as likely to reach $200k or more. However, **I found no source that breaks this down by genre** and none that controls for selection bias. Separately, the largest 2025 indie revenue hits appear to have come from outside the publisher system. That status comes from my general knowledge and needs checking.

### Cited Findings
- **Published versus self-published, 2024 releases** [HARD DATA, revenue estimates]:
  - The analysis comes from Over Powered Game Marketing. It covers more than 5,000 AA, indie and "hobbyist" Steam games released in 2024 (5,773 after filtering for 2024 release, AA/Indie/Hobbyist classification and at least $500 revenue).
  - Data sources are GameDiscoverCo and Gamalytic. "Hobbyist" means any publisher with less than $10k lifetime revenue on Steam.
  - Reported results: published games had **2× higher mean** and **5× higher median** revenue, were **4× more likely to earn $200k or more**, and **2× more likely to earn $5,000 or more**.
  - Sources: [OPG Substack](https://opgamemarketing.substack.com/p/the-2024-indie-and-aa-game-market); relayed by [VoxBooster (2026)](https://voxbooster.com/blog/indie-game-statistics-2026/)
  - **Attribution conflict:** [Superjump (2025)](https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/the-state-of-indie-games-in-2025-and-beyond-part-2/) attributes the "2× ≥$5k / 4× ≥$200k" figures to **VG Insights** data, while VoxBooster attributes them to the OPG analysis.
- The VG Insights (now Sensor Tower) entity labelled "Self-Published" shows 33 games, $1.1m lifetime revenue and a $396 median. It is a catch-all label, not representative of self-published games. — [Sensor Tower/VGI page](https://app.sensortower.com/vgi/publisher/2211/self-published)
- Market baseline for 2025 releases [HARD DATA, estimates]:
  - Alinea Analytics: indie games earned about 25% of Steam's roughly **$17.7bn** 2025 revenue. Only about **300 of roughly 20,000** 2025 releases earned more than $1m.
  - Alinea's top indie earners in 2025: Schedule I about $151m, R.E.P.O. about $147m, PEAK about $87m, Hollow Knight: Silksong about $75m, Escape from Duckov about $53m. Together these five made up about 3% of Steam's annual revenue.
  - Sources: [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/analytics/item-49645); [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Indie-games-accounted-for-25-of-Steam-s-revenue-in-2025.1189429.0.html)
- Gamalytic data for 2025 releases: about **40%** did not make back the $100 Steam Direct fee (more than 5,000 games). — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/over-5-000-games-released-on-steam-this-year-didnt-make-enough-money-to-recover-the-usd100-fee-to-put-a-game-on-valves-store-research-estimates/)
- Median gross revenue for a 2025 Steam game is about **$249**, according to an analysis using VG Insights and Alinea data. — [Ziva](https://ziva.sh/blogs/indie-game-revenue)
- How To Market A Game: **18,324** Steam releases in 2024, and **445** indie or AA developers passed 1,000 reviews (about 2.44%). — [GameDev Reports summary](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/how-to-market-a-game-steam-in-2024); [VoxBooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/indie-game-statistics-2026/)
- [OLDER DATA, Oct 2023] Gamalytic found half of the roughly 41k games released in the previous 3 years made **$500 or less** gross. — [Game World Observer, 6 Oct 2023](https://gameworldobserver.com/2023/10/06/steam-stats-41k-games-last-3-years-half-made-500-or-less)
- Methodology caveat: Gamalytic estimates revenue from concurrent players, a refined review multiplier and disclosed sales. It reports 77% of estimates within ±30% and 98% within ±50%. Accuracy is weaker for small and F2P games, and these accuracy tests are self-graded. — [Steam Page Analyzer comparison (2026)](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/gamalytic-vs-vg-insights)
- Devolver's front catalogue: US$37.9m from 15 new releases in 2025. This covers all platforms and is publisher revenue, not Steam gross [HARD DATA]. — [Devolver FY2025 announcement](https://investors.devolverdigital.com/files/downloads-and-publications/2026/devolver--full-year-2025-announcement.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Against the study's $300k "moderate success" bar:** the closest published threshold is $200k. On the OPG/VGI figures, a published game was about 4× as likely as a self-published one to clear it in 2024. The gap is probably inflated by **selection bias**, because publishers pick stronger projects; no source corrects for this.
- Devolver's average front-catalogue revenue per 2025 new release works out to roughly **$2.5m** ($37.9m ÷ 15). That is my calculation; it covers all platforms and a skewed distribution, not a median.
- The 2025 indie revenue leaders (Schedule I, R.E.P.O., PEAK, Silksong, Escape from Duckov) suggest the very top of the market was **not dominated by third-party indie publishers**. In my general knowledge most were self-published, but Escape from Duckov is believed to have had a publisher. **I did not verify any of this in this session.** The most visible publisher-backed 2025 hits were Clair Obscur (Kepler) and Ball x Pit (Devolver).

### Gaps
- **No genre-level split** of published versus self-published share, success rate or median revenue was found from Gamalytic, VG Insights, GameDiscoverCo or HTMAG.

## Q4. How did the 2023–2026 funding contraction change what gets signed? What are typical deal sizes, recoup and revenue-split terms, and signing criteria?

### Takeaway
The contraction meant **fewer signings, smaller cheques, more publishers shutting down or restructuring, and a much higher bar of proof before signing**: playable prototype or demo, wishlists, community, and evidence of long-tail potential. Most developers now self-fund. The best available deal dataset (Voyer Law, 2017–2025 agreements) puts the **median advance at $300k (mean about $675k)**. The median developer revenue share is **50% with an advance and 70% without**. Terms average about 6.8 years with an advance and 4.2 without.

### Inferences
- **Recoup arithmetic against the $300k bar** (my calculation, Steam only; it ignores VAT, refunds, other platforms and any marketing recoup). With the median advance of **$300k**:
  - **Publisher takes 100% until recoup** (the reported 42% of deals): the game needs about **$430k Steam gross**, because $300k ÷ 0.70 after Valve's 30% cut is roughly $430k, before the developer earns any royalty.
  - **Recoup out of the developer's 50% share:** it needs about **$860k gross**.
  - In other words, a game that "only" reaches $300k gross would typically **not recoup** a median advance. Publishers screening for traction is consistent with deals sized for outcomes well above $300k.
- The contraction seems to favour genres that show demand early (demos, wishlists, playtests) and earn over a long tail (updates, DLC). This links back to the Q2 inference.

## Q5. Which genres attract platform or investor funding?

### Takeaway
**Platform-holder money for indies has shrunk.** Game Pass and Epic exclusivity deals were reported smaller from 2024, and Xbox reportedly paused or slowed new third-party Game Pass deals around a mid-2026 "reset". These are developer and insider reports; Xbox has not confirmed them. The newer, **better-documented money comes from successful indie studios' own funds and labels**. Outersloth has put about $20m into 24 games; Pocketpair Publishing and Aggro Crab also sign games. These vehicles show a **broad genre spread**, including narrative, platformer, idle, horror and roguelike metroidvania. I retrieved no 2024–2026 genre data for Sony's China Hero Project, Tencent/NetEase, Kowloon Nights or Indie Fund.
